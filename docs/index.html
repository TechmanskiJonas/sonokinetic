Sonokinetic

Can motion in a field of sound be perceived without localizing anything in it?

Source README

A single piece of audio is used to build a ring of virtual sources around the listener. Each source is made individually too diffuse to localize, and the ring is then rotated. The question is whether a field whose contents cannot be located is still heard to move.

The variants below are rendered from the same twenty seconds of the same signal. Each one is aligned to the sample and matched in loudness against the untreated reference, so that moving between them varies the treatment while the material is held constant. The monitor shows source positions as recorded by the renderer during the render itself.

Headphones are required, since the method depends on interaural differences that survive only under headphone playback. Operating-system spatial audio should be disabled, as it applies a second head-related transfer function to signals that already carry one.

    Objective measurement

    Interaural cross-correlation (IACC) is the standard objective measure of how diffuse a binaural signal is, where 1.0 corresponds to a single coherent image and values near zero to full decorrelation. It is reported below for every render on this page.

    VariantMotionIACC, droneIACC, strings

    The two highlighted rows differ in whether the field rotates. Their IACC scores differ by 0.019 on the drone and 0.028 on the strings, while the measure itself varies by 0.038 across random seeds at a fixed configuration. The separation between a rotating field and a static one therefore falls below the measure's own variability on both beds. Establishing that a field is heard to move consequently requires a matched control and a listener who is blind to the condition, which is the arrangement implemented in the application accompanying this page.

    Scope

    The default head model is a rigid sphere, so front-back information is available only in the variant rendered through the measured HRTF. Distance is carried by level alone, without propagation delay, Doppler shift, air absorption or reverberation. The results reported here come from a single listener who is aware of the hypothesis. The full set of limitations is documented in the repository README.
